Who found the mug and the sink

Gemma did the language part. It read “bring the mug over to the sink” and chose pick(mug), then place(sink_counter). The geometry came from elsewhere:

  1. The mug's position comes from the simulator, which spawned it. look() hands Gemma that list as text.
  2. The sink counter is a named spot in a catalog built offline from the kitchen scan: a surface point, a standing pose 0.3 m away, and the nearest waypoint.
  3. Walking there is an A* route on the kitchen floor map, driven closed-loop on the robot's measured pose.
  4. The reach is IK aiming the palm at the object, executed by SONIC, then nudged until the palm is within a few cm.
  5. The grasp attaches the object to the wrist in the sim once the palm is close. No fingers close on it.

Can Gemma see them itself?

Not reliably yet. Four frames from the run were sent to Gemma 26B, asking for the mug and the sink.

  • It found the mug in 1 of 4 frames. Its description was right (“small black mug… left of the sink”), but the pixel it gave lands on the robot's leg (red circle).
  • It called the stove a sink (left frame).

The fix is an open-vocabulary detector for where things are, with Gemma choosing what to act on.

Real and shortcut

  • Real: every step and reach is SONIC under physics, and so are the waist lean and arm motion. The robot never fell.
  • Shortcut: the grasp. The object attaches to the wrist once the palm is close; objects are visual-only props.
  • Shortcut: perception. Object positions come from the sim, not the camera (see above).
  • Limit: SONIC follows the hand target with 4–8 cm error before correction, and won't bring the palm much below 0.9 m. Low surfaces are marginal.
